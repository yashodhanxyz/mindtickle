# Design decisions

My first thought was to use Mobbin MCP for research. I got it connected, but unfortunately it was not of much use for what I was trying to build. I also wanted to see how far I could push AI, so I asked it to research, think through the requirement, and come up with options. That approach failed miserably in the first round. All three options were basically the user asking a question and getting an answer. There was no real conversation.

I stepped back and broke the user journey into smaller parts: asking, reading the reply, checking the evidence, asking a follow-up, and going back to work. That is how I arrived at two options: a floating chat that can be minimised, and a third column beside the main content. I wanted Jordan to continue the conversation and return to it without starting again.

One decision I particularly like is where the evidence lives. Initially, the control came after the next step, which felt out of place. I moved it beside the skill assessment, with evidence opening under each score. You can quickly read the answer and then inspect what supports it.

AI helped with implementation and checking the work, but I had to keep reviewing the actual experience. I pushed back on the one-way concepts, unnecessary chat branding, and awkward interactions. Working on smaller parts gave me much better results than asking AI to solve everything together.

Next, I would test both options with users to understand which fits their workflow better, and spend more time checking the streaming, keyboard experience, and behaviour on actual phones.
